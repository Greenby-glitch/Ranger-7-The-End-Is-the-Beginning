How to read the final sequence

The opening globe is a 3D illustration using a modern LRO colour mosaic. The later views are historic Ranger 7 still photographs. Their animated zooms are an educational reconstruction, not original film or an exact simulation of the flight path.

The sequence compresses time and switches between camera fields. Frame A199 was taken about 2.5 seconds before impact. The P200 plate preserves partially transmitted frames from about 0.39 and 0.19 seconds before impact. NASA's catalog explains that the transmissions were cut off by the collision.

NASA Science publishes a total of 4,316 images; NASA History and JPL publish 4,308. The main story says "more than 4,300." Design values from the 1965 Ranger 8/9 record are identified as related Block III specifications.

Project, artwork and AI credits

Human contribution. EBA-07 comprises Emiliano, Bryan and Alejandra. The team conducted and organized the documentary research, developed the story's structure and scenes, wrote and reviewed the scientific explanations, and selected the NASA/JPL sources. Emiliano had already developed an initial working codebase. The team also contributed directly to the programming, editing, integration and creative decisions.

Artificial intelligence disclosure. AI tools assisted with the creation, editing and organization of some portions of the HTML, CSS and JavaScript, as well as initial drafts of the educational quiz questions and feedback. These contributions were reviewed, adapted and integrated by people; the project was not developed exclusively by AI. The EBA-07 emblem and the EBA robot mascot are AI-generated artwork, with creative direction and selection by the team. The two animated quiz reactions were generated with AI and are visibly labeled. Some technical schematics use AI-assisted implementation and carry a visible attribution. Other illustrations are educational reconstructions; none is an original photograph taken by Ranger 7. Original NASA imagery is credited separately.

Photography and scientific sources. Historical photographs, genuine mission images, official engineering drawings and NASA lunar mosaics retain their individual source credits in the gallery above. An animated presentation of authentic images is an educational reconstruction, not recovered mission video. No NASA endorsement or validation of this independent project is implied.

Interactive guide and voice. EBA uses predefined responses, not a live generative AI chatbot. Optional spoken narration and quiz feedback use the visitor's browser speech-synthesis service, not AI-generated voice recordings.
